Detailed Description

Volume shadow copies read raw: the catalog and stores that the Volume Shadow Copy service keeps on an NTFS volume, and each copy rebuilt as the volume was when it was taken — without the service.

WHY RAW. A copy read through the service would make WAC call it, mount devices and leave its traces on the examined machine; the copies are data on the volume, which WAC reads raw like the rest (raw_hive.h).

THE FORMAT, from Joachim Metz's documentation of libvshadow and its sources (Vista and later; Windows XP keeps no persistent copy). At 0x1E00 of the volume, a header gives the CATALOG: blocks of 16 KiB whose entries describe each copy (type 2: volume size, store identifier, creation time) and where its store lies (type 3). A STORE keeps the previous content of the 16 KiB blocks written after its copy was taken: a list of block descriptors (original block -> copy on the volume), with forwarders (the block is in a newer store), overlays (some 512-byte sectors only) and ignored entries, and two bitmaps of the blocks unused by the copy.

A copy is read by applying the stores from the most recent down to its own over the current volume: a block its store does not hold is read from the newer stores, then from the volume. The algorithm is libvshadow's, step for step, so that the two read the same bytes — libvshadow is the independent reference of the tests.

Portable C++ without windows.h: the volume is read through a callback, and the module is checked on an image of a real volume (vss_test).
