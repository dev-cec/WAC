Detailed Description

Classic event logs (.evt) of Windows XP and Server 2003, read into the same XML as the .evtx records.

THE FORMAT. A header of 0x30 bytes — its size, "LfLe", version 1.1, the offsets of the oldest record and of the end-of-file record, the next and the oldest record numbers, the maximum size, flags, retention — then a circular area of records. Each record: its length, "LfLe", its number, the time it was generated and written (Unix seconds, UTC), the event identifier (its low 16 bits; the high 16, the qualifiers), its type, number of strings, category, the offsets and lengths of the user's SID, the strings and the binary data, then the source and computer names (UTF-16, null-terminated), the SID, the strings, the data, and its length again. A record that reaches the end of the file goes on right after the header. The walk starts at the oldest record and ends at the end-of-file record (0x28 bytes, 0x11111111 … 0x44444444).

WHY THE XML OF AN .EVTX. A record becomes the XML Windows Vista and later render for a classic source (Provider Name, EventID and its Qualifiers, Level, Task, TimeCreated, EventRecordID, Channel, Computer, Security UserID, EventData with its Data and its Binary): events.cpp builds the event, its message (from the source's message files declared in SYSTEM) and its JSON as for an .evtx — one events.json, one form.

Every offset and length comes from the file: each is bounded by the record, the record by the file; a forged length ends the walk, the records before kept.
